Why fuzz has a cone angle

Every other softness control is called roughness; PxrSurface's fuzz asks for degrees. That's not a naming quirk — the fuzz lobe has no mirror direction for a roughness to widen, and Arnold's sheen is a different model rather than the same one in other units.

How to use these guides

Each guide opens with the mental model — the one or two ideas that make everything else make sense — followed by a quick-reference table for looking up a single control fast. After that it goes group by group through the controls, with a plain-language explanation, a real-world analogy, and a callout wherever something behaves differently than you'd expect. You don't need to read them front to back; jump to the section you need.

Parameter names, defaults and conversion factors are checked against the actual shader definitions rather than written from memory, so the numbers are safe to act on — though it's always worth a sanity check against the versions installed in your own pipeline.

Most guides carry interactive widgets — live spheres, curves and diagrams you can drag. They compute the real maths in your browser, so they are good for building intuition about how a control behaves, but they are not a substitute for a test render in your own scene.
